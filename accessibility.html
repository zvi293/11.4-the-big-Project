01My Commitment

I want everyone to be able to read this site, operate it, and get in touch — whatever device, browser or assistive technology they use. Accessibility here is not a bolt-on: it is part of how the site is designed and hand-coded, and part of the "Refinement & care" work I offer clients.

02Conformance Target

This website aims to conform to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2, Level AA. The guidelines are published by the W3C and explain how to make web content more accessible to people with a wide range of disabilities, including visual, motor, cognitive and vestibular conditions.

03What Is Built In

  • Semantic structure — real headings in order, landmarks (header, navigation, main, footer), lists and native elements, so screen readers can map the page.
  • Full keyboard support — every menu, dialog, accordion, tab, filter and form control works with a keyboard alone, with a visible focus outline and a "Skip to content" link.
  • Accessible dialogs — the menu and study windows are native dialogs: focus is trapped while open, Escape and the phone's back button close them, and focus returns to where you were.
  • Colour contrast — text colours are chosen to meet or exceed the WCAG AA contrast ratios on both the dark and paper grounds.
  • Labelled forms — every field has a visible label, errors are announced and described next to the field they belong to, and the form can be completed without a mouse.
  • Text alternatives — meaningful images carry descriptive alt text; decorative artwork is hidden from assistive technology.
  • Resizable text — the layout uses fluid units and stays usable when text is enlarged or the page is zoomed to 200%.
  • No traps — no autoplaying sound, no flashing content, no time limits on reading or on the contact form.

04Motion & Animation

A real-time animated ribbon runs through this site. It is decorative only: it is hidden from assistive technology, and no information exists only inside it.

  • If your system asks for reduced motion (prefers-reduced-motion), the site loads with motion off: no animation, no parallax, no moving reveals.
  • A motion toggle sits in the navigation on every visit, so you can pause or resume all movement yourself. Your choice is remembered on your device.
  • Browsers without WebGL, and forced-colours / high-contrast modes, automatically get a still version of the page with the same content.

05Known Limitations

  • The three concept-study frames contain small pieces of invented interface artwork (a fictional menu, product card and dashboard). They are decorative and hidden from screen readers; each study's real name and description sit beside it as text.
  • The hero headline is redrawn inside the animated scene for sighted visitors. The original text stays in the page for screen readers, search engines and text selection.

If you find anything else that gets in your way, I would genuinely like to know — see the contact section below.

06Compatibility & Testing

The site is tested on current versions of Chrome, Safari, Firefox and Edge, on Windows, macOS, Android and iOS, at phone, tablet and desktop sizes. Checks combine automated audits (Lighthouse, axe) with manual keyboard and screen-reader passes, and the site is re-checked when meaningful changes ship.
